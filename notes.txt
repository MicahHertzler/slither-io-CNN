Turn the observations into design choices
Use the player’s head as the screen origin. crop around it and represent nearby snakes and pellets relative to that point.

Make steering a ring of actions. Define, say, 16 or 32 direction options around the center. 
The policy picks a direction, and your controller moves the cursor to a point along that ray.
 Add boost as a separate action. Active boost increases turning radius.  

Use outlines to detect snakes. Low graphics and consistent zoom can make the outlines easier to process.
 Treat outlines as a helpful cue, then check how well they hold up when snakes overlap.

Handle eye differences as a cue, not a requirement. 
Eye positions from the skin-selection screen could help identify the selected skin or estimate head orientation. 
Since eyes vary, keep another cue—such as head shape and body direction—for cases where eye detection fails.
Keep the visual input consistent. Use the same graphics setting, window size, and crop during training and play.
